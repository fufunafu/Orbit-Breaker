# Orbit Breaker iOS Prototype Plan

## Summary

Create a fast, portrait-mode iPhone game in the existing Godot 4.7.2 project. The player taps to launch a ship from one orbiting planet to the next, earning points until a missed jump or hazard ends the run.

## Core Game

- Use a 1080 x 1920 portrait reference resolution with responsive scaling and safe-area-aware UI.
- Implement four states: READY, ORBITING, IN_FLIGHT, and GAME_OVER.
- While orbiting, the ship moves automatically around the current planet.
- A screen tap launches the ship along its current tangent at a fixed speed.
- Touching the highlighted target planet captures the ship into a new orbit.
- Missing the target, leaving the play area, exceeding the flight timeout, or touching a hazard ends the run.
- Restart immediately from the game-over screen with one tap.
- Generate reachable planets continuously above the player and move the camera upward after successful landings.
- Begin adding hazards after 10 landings.
- Increase orbital speed, reduce average planet size, and tighten placement windows every 5 landings.
- Award one point multiplied by the current combo. A trajectory passing through the target planet's inner perfect zone increases the combo, up to 5x. A normal landing resets it to 1x.
- Store the best score locally between sessions.

## Presentation and Technical Structure

- Build the game from Godot scenes for the game controller, ship, planets, hazards, effects, and HUD.
- Use procedural circles, gradients, glow, trails, particles, camera shake, and screen flashes for the neon-space style. No external artwork is required for the prototype.
- Display the current score, best score, combo, start prompt, and game-over panel.
- Add short launch, landing, perfect-hit, and failure sounds. Include light iPhone haptics for launches, stronger feedback for perfect landings, and failure feedback.
- Pause safely when the app moves into the background and resume without changing the current run.
- Define a launch input action shared by touch, mouse click, and Space for editor testing.
- Use a GameState enum and signals for run_started, landed, perfect_landed, score_changed, and run_ended.
- Save data in user://save.cfg with a version number and best_score integer. Missing or invalid data starts at zero without blocking play.
- Configure the app as Orbit Breaker, bundle identifier com.antonio.orbitbreaker, portrait only, and iOS 16 or newer.
- Install matching Godot 4.7.2 export templates, export an Xcode project, and use the installed Xcode 26.6 toolchain for iPhone builds.
- Keep monetization, accounts, leaderboards, achievements, cosmetics, levels, advertisements, and App Store submission outside this prototype.

## Test and Acceptance Plan

- Verify launches use the correct tangent in both clockwise and counterclockwise orbits.
- Confirm every generated target has at least one valid launch window and unreachable layouts are rejected.
- Test normal landings, perfect landings, combo growth, combo reset, difficulty steps, hazards, misses, and flight timeout.
- Confirm rapid taps cannot launch twice or restart into an invalid state.
- Confirm the best score persists after closing and reopening the app and corrupt save data fails safely.
- Test touch controls and readable safe-area UI on small and large iPhone screen sizes.
- Confirm backgrounding does not end or advance a run unexpectedly.
- Maintain stable performance at 60 FPS with particles and several planets visible.
- Complete at least ten consecutive full runs in the Godot editor without errors.
- Export successfully to Xcode and complete at least one run on an iPhone or iOS simulator.
- On a physical iPhone, verify portrait locking, touch response, audio, haptics, pause behavior, and instant restart.

## Assumptions

- The first milestone is a playable prototype, not a public App Store release.
- Runs are endless, use one-hit failure, and focus entirely on high-score replayability.
- The existing Godot project remains the implementation base.
- A free or paid Apple developer team and a connected iPhone may be needed for physical-device signing.
- Difficulty constants will be exposed in one configuration resource so they can be tuned without rewriting gameplay code.
